# Heathrow: 80 years in 60 seconds (1946–2026)

This is a 60-second vertical video, 1080×1920 at 60 fps with sound, showing how London
Heathrow grew from its first departure in 1946 to today. It is made up of five sections:

- **The schematic plan:** the year rolls on an odometer while the airport changes. Runways
  open, close and get longer. Terminals, the cargo area and the Piccadilly line appear.
- **Panels under the plan:** a passengers-per-year counter and chart, counters for
  runways, terminals and years open, and a card for each milestone.
- **Top routes:** a world map of where Heathrow flies most (2024 passengers), plus the
  1946 first flight to Buenos Aires.
- **What's next:** the site's size today and the proposed third runway.
- **Finale:** 1946 compared with today.

## Limits

- **Passengers 1952–1985.** These are drawn between the published milestones: 1951, more
  than 2m by 1958, 14m in 1968, below 19.4m in 1972, and 27m by 1979. The counter marks
  those years "≈ ESTIMATE".
- **Runway closure years.** The individual diagonal-runway closure years (1959, 1965,
  1972) are approximate. Sources only say the diagonals closed during the 1950s–70s.
- **Airlines and destinations.** Year-by-year counts are not published, so the video
  only compares 1946 with today.
- **The plan.** It is a schematic, not a survey: positions and sizes are approximate.
